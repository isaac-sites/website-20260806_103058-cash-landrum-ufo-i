## Introduction

The Bergstrom Air Force Base interview of Betty Cash, [Vickie Landrum]({{ 'vickie-landrum/' | relative_url }}) and [Colby Landrum]({{ 'colby-landrum/' | relative_url }}) is one of the most valuable records in the Cash–Landrum case because it captures the witnesses speaking at length only eight months after the reported encounter. ## What the interview actually fixed in the record

## Colby’s testimony was recorded, but not independently developed

Colby Landrum was interviewed during the same session about what he saw and about his health. His inclusion is significant because it preserves the child witness’s voice rather than relying entirely on adult summaries. The questioning, however, occurred with his grandmother and Cash present, after the event had already been discussed repeatedly within the family and with investigators.

The transcript records Colby answering simple questions about physical symptoms, including skin eruptions and continuing stomach discomfort. It does not amount to a structured forensic interview designed to test whether his visual memories were independent of the adults’ accounts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/cashlandrumint02.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It has been claimed that the craft was a US attempt at making a UFO since it was spewing out flames at the bottom and was joined by many...</span></span></span>

This limits how the recording should be used. Colby’s participation corroborates that he was presented to Air Force personnel as a witness and that he reported distress. It cannot by itself demonstrate that each descriptive detail attributed to him originated independently, nor does it resolve whether later similarities among the three narratives reflect shared observation, repeated discussion or both.

## The transcript is a source, not an official finding

The text commonly circulated online was transcribed years later from a copy of the recording reportedly supplied by [Betty Cash]({{ 'betty-cash/' | relative_url }}) to UFO researchers. The transcriber expressly warned that some words and names were uncertain, that comments about pauses or vocal emphasis were interpretative, and that no greater claim of accuracy should be inferred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/cashlandrumint01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It has been claimed that the craft was a US attempt at making a UFO since it was spewing out flames at the bottom and was joined by many...</span></span></span>

## How the recording shaped later versions

The Bergstrom interview helped stabilise the features that became standard in later narratives: a diamond-shaped object, flame beneath it, intense heat, an estimated [23 helicopters]({{ '23-helicopters/' | relative_url }}), reported medical effects and the belief that the object was probably human-made. Because these elements appeared in a recorded government meeting, later writers could cite them as early documented testimony rather than folklore added many years afterwards.

At the same time, repeated summaries tended to strip away the interview’s hesitations. “About 23” became “23”; helicopters resembling twin-rotor military types became specifically identified Chinooks; uncertain or disputed lettering became clear United States markings; and a meeting with base legal officers was sometimes described as though the Air Force had conducted a formal UFO investigation.

The witnesses’ own discussion of possible explanations was also more complicated than a simple extraterrestrial claim. Landrum said she believed the object had to be man-made and rejected the idea of “little green men”. Cash similarly said she had not previously believed in life from other planets. Their suspicion of government responsibility arose primarily from the helicopters and their injuries, not from a claim that they had recognised a known [secret aircraft]({{ 'secret-aircraft/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 2 of 2TRANSCRIPT OF BERGSTROM AFB INTERVIEW OF ... One particularly instructive episode concerned a National Guard helicopter crewman whom Landrum believed had mentioned being called out for a UFO incident. Later examination of a separate recording indicates that the pilot was referring to an earlier event, apparently in 1977, rather than confirming deployment on the night of 29 December 1980. This shows how an ambiguous conversation could become attached to the central allegation as the story developed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.blueblurrylines.com/2022/10/the-cash-landrum-ufo-1980s-recording-of.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blueblurrylines.com">[Blue Blurry Lines]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blueblurrylines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blueblurrylines.com.</span></span></span>

## What can safely be concluded

The Bergstrom recording demonstrates that Cash, Landrum and Colby personally presented a detailed account to Air Force legal personnel on 17 August 1981. It fixes an early version of their reported time, route, object description, helicopter estimate, illnesses and financial concerns. It also records their frustration at being unable to identify a government body that would investigate or accept responsibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.whipnet.com/sites/ufo.whipnet.org/xdocs/Cash.Landrum/trans1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whipnet.com">[Whipnet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whipnet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO.Whipnet.org &#124; Aliens &#124; UFO &#124; The Piney Woods UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aliens &#124; UFO &#124; The Piney Woods UFO - Transcript 1UFO.Whipnet.org &#124; Aliens &#124; UFO &#124; The Piney Woods UFO - Transcript 1 Documents Photos R...</span></span></span>

It does not demonstrate that an unidentified craft was military property, that 23 helicopters were present, that any aircraft carried Air Force markings, or that radiation caused the reported illnesses. On those central questions, the interview preserves allegations rather than resolving them.

Its greatest evidential value lies precisely in that distinction. The recording is stronger than a late anecdotal retelling because it captures the witnesses directly and allows their wording to be examined. It is weaker than physical, operational or scientific evidence because it contains no corroborating flight records, radar data, photographs of the encounter, verified markings or measured exposure. Read carefully, the Bergstrom interview neither dismisses the witnesses nor proves their interpretation. It preserves what they said while revealing how much remained uncertain even at an early stage of the case.
