## Introduction

The Bergstrom Air Force Base interview did not establish the exact location of the reported Cash–Landrum encounter because the witnesses and Air Force personnel were working from a map that was too general for the task. Rather than identifying a precise point on Farm-to-Market Road 1485, the recorded session narrowed the incident only to the wider area between New Caney and Huffman, northeast of Houston. That distinction is important because later accounts sometimes imply that the interview fixed a specific site when the recording itself shows the opposite. The tape documents both what the witnesses could confidently identify and the practical limits imposed by the map available during the meeting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/cashlandrumint01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: the Cash-Landrum case, 1980, USAF interview part IJune 4, 1996...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 4, 1996</span></span></span>

## The map used during the interview

Very early in the interview, Captain John Camp asked [Betty Cash]({{ 'betty-cash/' | relative_url }}) whether she could indicate where the encounter had occurred on a Texas map placed before her. Cash first noted that her eyesight remained poor, remarking that she had required several pairs of glasses since the incident. After a pause while she and [Vickie Landrum]({{ 'vickie-landrum/' | relative_url }}) examined the map together, the recording breaks briefly.

When the tape resumes, Camp explicitly explains what had happened. He states that the recording had been paused to allow Cash to locate the site, but that "because of the lack of detail of the map" she had been unable to identify the exact location. This statement comes from the interviewer himself rather than from later commentary, making it one of the clearest pieces of evidence about why the location could not be fixed during the session.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Transcript%20of%20taped%20interview%20held%20at%20Bergstom%20Air%20Force%20Base%20Law%20Library%2C%2017%20August%201981.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview...</span></span></span>

The problem therefore was not presented as an inability to remember the general route. Instead, it was a practical cartographic limitation: a statewide Texas map lacked the local roads, landmarks and scale necessary to pinpoint a short stretch of rural highway.

## What Cash could and could not identify

Although the witnesses could not indicate an exact spot, they consistently identified the broader geographical setting.

They stated that the encounter had occurred:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* on Farm-to-Market Road 1485;
* between New Caney and Huffman;
* northeast of Houston;
* in the vicinity of Montgomery County rather than at their own homes in Liberty County.

As the discussion continued, Cash and Landrum worked together over the map, identifying nearby reference points such as New Caney, Huffman and Humble (transcribed in some versions as "Humboldt"). They also corrected confusion over county boundaries, distinguishing where they lived from where they believed the incident had taken place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Transcript%20of%20taped%20interview%20held%20at%20Bergstom%20Air%20Force%20Base%20Law%20Library%2C%2017%20August%201981.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview...</span></span></span>

## Why the missing pinpoint matters

The inability to mark a precise location has several consequences for evaluating later claims about the case.

First, it limits the certainty with which investigators can associate later physical evidence with the reported encounter. Claims involving a specific patch of road, nearby property or exact coordinates cannot be traced directly to the Bergstrom interview because no such location was established during that recorded session.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Transcript%20of%20taped%20interview%20held%20at%20Bergstom%20Air%20Force%20Base%20Law%20Library%2C%2017%20August%201981.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview...</span></span></span>

Second, it complicates retrospective investigations. Researchers attempting to reconstruct the route years later have had to rely on witness recollections, later site visits and independent reconstructions rather than on an exact location preserved in the original Air Force interview.

Third, it illustrates the distinction between an early primary source and later narrative development. Some later retellings present a more definite incident site than the interview itself supports. The recording instead preserves a much more cautious historical record: the witnesses identified the road and surrounding communities, but not a precise point along that road.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gpposner.com/Cash-Landrum-chapter.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gpposner.com">[Gary P. ## How later site claims relate to the interview

Subsequent investigators, particularly within civilian UFO research, attempted to reconstruct the encounter location through field visits and witness-guided inspections. Those efforts produced maps and suggested locations along FM 1485, but they represent later investigative work rather than information fixed during the August 1981 Air Force interview.

Historical analyses of early case files note that even these later visits often referred to where Vickie Landrum believed they had stopped, rather than documenting an independently verified location. ## What the interview actually establishes

The Bergstrom recording provides a clear evidential boundary.

It establishes that:
