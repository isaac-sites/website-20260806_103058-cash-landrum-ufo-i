## Introduction

The Bergstrom Air Force Base interview is one of the earliest extended records in which [Betty Cash]({{ 'betty-cash/' | relative_url }}) described when the alleged Cash–Landrum encounter occurred. Rather than fixing the event to a single minute, the recording preserves a noticeably cautious estimate. Cash told the Air Force that the incident happened **"approximately between nine and nine-thirty at night"** on 29 December 1980 while travelling on Farm-to-Market Road 1485 between New Caney and Huffman. That half-hour window is historically significant because it shows that the witnesses themselves were not claiming a precisely established time only eight months after the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 1 of 2August 17, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 17, 1981</span></span></span>

## Was the Encounter Time Ever Precisely Established?

The Bergstrom recording does not establish an exact clock time for the encounter. Captain John Camp's opening questions invited Betty Cash to describe the date, place and circumstances, but when she reached the timing of the event she qualified it with an approximation rather than a definitive hour. Her wording was not corrected or narrowed during the interview, nor did the Air Force officers attempt to obtain a more exact minute-by-minute chronology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 1 of 2August 17, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 17, 1981</span></span></span>

This matters because witness recollections are often strongest when they preserve uncertainty instead of replacing it with false precision. The transcript reflects a natural estimate consistent with someone recalling an evening journey months after the event, rather than someone consulting a contemporaneous written record or verified timestamp.

## How Later Retellings Hardened an Approximate Time

As the Cash–Landrum case entered books, television programmes and internet summaries, the original wording often became simplified. Many secondary accounts state that the encounter occurred "at about 9.00 pm" or simply "around 9 pm." While these formulations are not necessarily inconsistent with Cash's statement, they subtly convert an expressed time range into what reads like a single reference point.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

That shift illustrates a common pattern in historical retellings. A rounded estimate is gradually repeated as though it were a fixed fact because concise narratives favour specific times over qualified ones. Readers encountering only later summaries may therefore assume that investigators established an exact chronology when the earliest recorded interview shows otherwise.

The Bergstrom transcript serves as an important corrective because it preserves the witnesses' own phrasing before decades of repetition standardised the story.

## What the Uncertainty Means for Timeline Reconstruction

The half-hour window has practical consequences for anyone attempting to compare the reported encounter with external records.

A reconstruction based on the Bergstrom interview should recognise that:

* the witnesses did not identify a precise minute for the beginning of the event;
* any comparison with aircraft activity, military operations, emergency logs or weather observations must accommodate a [time window]({{ 'time-window/' | relative_url }}) rather than a single timestamp;
* later analyses claiming close correspondence with events at exactly 9.00 pm should be evaluated against the broader estimate preserved in the interview itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 1 of 2August 17, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 17, 1981</span></span></span>

This does not invalidate either the witnesses' account or later investigative efforts. Instead, it defines the limits of what the primary source can support. Historical reconstruction is strongest when it distinguishes between what was actually recorded in 1981 and what later authors inferred or standardised.

## Why the Bergstrom Recording Remains the Key Reference

Among the earliest surviving extended witness accounts, the Bergstrom interview captures Betty Cash describing the encounter before decades of retelling had shaped the narrative. On the specific question of timing, its value lies less in establishing an exact hour than in documenting that no exact hour was claimed.

Consequently, the recording should be regarded as the primary reference for the encounter's timing: it supports an estimated window of **approximately 9.00 to 9.30 pm**, not a precisely established timestamp. That distinction is small in appearance but important in historical analysis because it preserves the original level of certainty expressed by the principal witness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 1 of 2August 17, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 17, 1981</span></span></span>

    });

      label.textContent = selectedText;

    });

      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;

    });

    }
    closeMenu(section);

        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });

    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');

        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
