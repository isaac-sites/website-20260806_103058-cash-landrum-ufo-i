## Introduction

Betty Cash's reported hair loss and skin injuries became the defining physical evidence in the Cash–Landrum incident. Unlike descriptions of the unidentified object itself, photographs of Cash's swollen face, patchy baldness and inflamed skin gave the story a tangible human dimension. Those injuries have been repeatedly cited by supporters as evidence that she experienced an unusually severe physical exposure. At the same time, later reviews of her [medical records]({{ 'medical-record/' | relative_url }}) have challenged whether the documented findings support the popular narrative of radiation burns or acute [radiation sickness]({{ 'radiation-claim/' | relative_url }}). The result is a distinction that runs throughout the case: there is little dispute that Cash became seriously ill, but there is substantial disagreement over how accurately later accounts describe her visible injuries and what caused them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

## Early Swelling and Skin Complaints

According to [Betty Cash]({{ 'betty-cash/' | relative_url }})'s own account, symptoms began within hours of the encounter. She described burning eyes, nausea, weakness and skin discomfort resembling severe sunburn. Over the following days, swelling reportedly increased around her face and scalp, prompting hospital admission in early January 1981. Later UFO literature often summarised this period as one of extensive burns and skin loss.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

The surviving medical evidence, however, presents a more nuanced picture. Reviews of the hospital records indicate that physicians documented marked swelling and inflammation, particularly of the scalp and face, but later critics have pointed out that dermatology consultations described [cellulitis]({{ 'cellulitis/' | relative_url }}) and soft-tissue swelling rather than widespread radiation burns. This distinction matters because later retellings frequently describe dramatic burn injuries that are less clearly reflected in the original clinical notes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

Even investigators sympathetic to the case acknowledged that doctors struggled to explain her condition. Multiple specialists were reportedly consulted because the combination of facial swelling, pain and systemic illness did not immediately fit an obvious diagnosis. The uncertainty of those examinations has contributed both to the case's enduring appeal and to continuing disagreement over what the medical record actually demonstrates.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufomagazines.com/apro-bulletin-apro-bulletin-1981-08-00-vol-29-no-8/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufomagazines.com">[UFO Magazine Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufomagazines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ufomagazines.com.</span></span></span>

## When Hair Loss Became Evident

[Hair loss]({{ 'hair-loss/' | relative_url }}) became the most recognisable feature of Betty Cash's illness because it was documented photographically and remained visible long after the initial swelling subsided. Images of large bald patches appeared in documentaries, books and newspaper coverage, making [alopecia]({{ 'alopecia/' | relative_url }}) the visual symbol of the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

One important point often overlooked is timing. Popular summaries sometimes imply that Cash arrived at hospital already losing large amounts of hair immediately after the encounter. Later reviews of the medical records argue that this chronology is inaccurate. They note that her attending physician initially recorded little, if any, hair loss on admission and that the more obvious bald patches developed during the following weeks rather than at the very beginning of treatment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

This does not mean the hair loss was insignificant. Contemporary photographs show extensive patchy alopecia that would have been physically and emotionally distressing. Instead, the disagreement concerns whether the progression matches later claims of sudden radiation-induced hair loss or whether it followed a different medical process. That distinction has become central to later evaluations of the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

## Claims the Medical Notes Do Not Confirm

Many popular retellings state that Betty Cash suffered classic radiation burns and that doctors eventually concluded she had radiation sickness. The publicly discussed medical documentation is less definitive.

Several later medical reviews argue that the records do not contain a diagnosis of acute radiation syndrome. Critics have also noted that doses of ionising radiation sufficient to produce the rapid onset of severe skin injury and extensive hair loss described in some narratives would normally be expected to produce far more serious, often fatal, systemic effects. Because Cash survived for many years after the incident, some researchers concluded that the medical picture does not fit known patterns of massive radiation exposure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

Another point of contention concerns the cause of the hair loss itself. Later analyses of the physicians' notes report that doctors attributed the bald patches to [alopecia areata]({{ 'alopecia-areata/' | relative_url }}), an autoimmune condition characterised by patchy hair loss. This interpretation does not explain every symptom Cash reported, but it does provide an alternative explanation for one of the incident's most famous visible injuries. Supporters of the original account dispute that conclusion, arguing that the overall pattern of illness cannot be reduced to a single diagnosis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

Similarly, descriptions of extensive skin peeling or loss have become embedded in many secondary accounts, yet reviewers of the available records argue that the documentation is stronger for swelling, inflammation and cellulitis than for the dramatic burn narrative often repeated in UFO literature. ## Why the Visible Injuries Remain Central to the Case

Betty Cash's hair loss and skin condition continue to occupy a unique place in discussions of the Cash–Landrum incident because they are among the few aspects supported by tangible evidence such as photographs, hospital admission and medical treatment. They distinguish the case from many UFO reports that rely entirely on witness testimony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>
