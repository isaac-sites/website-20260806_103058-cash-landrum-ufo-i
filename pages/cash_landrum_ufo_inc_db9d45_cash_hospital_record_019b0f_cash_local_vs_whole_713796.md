## Introduction

One of the central medical questions in the Cash–Landrum case is not simply whether [Betty Cash]({{ 'betty-cash/' | relative_url }}) experienced symptoms that can occur after radiation exposure, but whether those symptoms are more compatible with **localised radiation injury** or **whole-body acute radiation syndrome (ARS)**. This distinction matters because severe skin injury, swelling and hair loss can occur after a concentrated dose to a limited area of the body without producing the characteristic blood-cell abnormalities and systemic organ damage expected after high-dose whole-body irradiation. Modern radiation medicine recognises these as related but distinct clinical patterns. Applying that distinction to Cash's surviving [medical record]({{ 'medical-record/' | relative_url }}) suggests that a local exposure mechanism is more medically plausible than classical whole-body ARS if ionising radiation were involved at all. However, the available evidence is insufficient to reconstruct where any dose was delivered, how intense it was, or even whether radiation was the cause.

## How local radiation injury differs from whole-body exposure

Radiation injuries depend not only on the total amount of radiation absorbed but also on **how that dose is distributed throughout the body**.

Whole-body acute radiation syndrome develops only when a sufficiently high dose of penetrating radiation reaches most of the body's internal organs over a short period. Damage to rapidly dividing cells—especially in the bone marrow, gastrointestinal tract and skin—produces a recognisable sequence of illness including early nausea or vomiting, followed by characteristic declines in blood-cell counts and, at higher doses, infection, bleeding and multiple organ complications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/hcp/clinical-guidance/ars.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdc.gov">[CDC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Acute Radiation Syndrome: Information for Clinicians &#124; Radiation Emergencies &#124; CDCApril 23, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 23, 2024</span></span></span>

By contrast, **cutaneous radiation injury** or **local radiation injury** results when a high dose is concentrated in a limited region. In these cases:

* redness and swelling may appear within hours or days;
* a symptom-free interval can follow;
* blistering, ulceration or [delayed skin]({{ 'delayed-skin/' | relative_url }}) damage may emerge weeks later;
* hair follicles within the irradiated area may stop producing hair, causing localised epilation.

Importantly, these injuries can occur **without classical acute radiation syndrome**, because the rest of the body's tissues receive little or no damaging dose. The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) specifically notes that cutaneous radiation injury can occur without ARS, particularly after partial-body exposures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/hcp/clinical-guidance/cri.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdc.gov">[CDC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cutaneous Radiation Injury (CRI): Information for Clinicians &#124; Radiation Emergencies &#124; CDCApril 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 17, 2024</span></span></span>

This distinction has become increasingly important in modern radiation emergency medicine because many accidental exposures involve only part of the body rather than uniform irradiation.

## Could Betty Cash's recorded symptoms fit local irradiation?

Several features documented during Cash's hospitalisation have often been cited as compatible with local radiation injury.

The medical records describe marked swelling of the scalp, forehead and eyelids together with crusted scalp lesions and later pronounced [hair loss]({{ 'hair-loss/' | relative_url }}). Modern descriptions of cutaneous radiation injury include oedema, erythema, delayed skin breakdown and epilation as recognised effects of substantial local doses. Hair loss commonly appears after a delay because radiation first damages actively dividing cells within hair follicles before visible shedding occurs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/hcp/clinical-guidance/cri.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdc.gov">[CDC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cutaneous Radiation Injury (CRI): Information for Clinicians &#124; Radiation Emergencies &#124; CDCApril 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 17, 2024</span></span></span>

The delayed timing of Cash's [alopecia]({{ 'alopecia/' | relative_url }}) therefore does not, by itself, argue either for or against radiation. It is consistent with the biological timing expected after significant skin irradiation, but it is also compatible with several non-radiation conditions affecting the scalp.

Equally significant is the apparent concentration of symptoms around the face and scalp. If radiation exposure had occurred from a directional source, a non-uniform dose could theoretically produce severe injury in one anatomical region while sparing much of the rest of the body. Such uneven dose distributions are well documented in industrial radiation accidents and radiotherapy misadministrations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8785213/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed Central (PMC)]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pub Med Central (PMC)Cutaneous and Local Radiation Injuries</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PubMed Central (PMC)Cutaneous and Local Radiation Injuries - PMC...</span></span></span>

However, "compatible with" does not mean "diagnostic of". Swelling, inflammatory skin changes and hair loss all have numerous alternative medical explanations, and none uniquely identify radiation as the cause.

## Why the records do not resemble classical whole-body acute radiation syndrome

The strongest argument against interpreting Cash's illness as straightforward whole-body ARS is not the presence of skin injury but the absence of several findings expected after a sufficiently large uniform dose.

Current clinical guidance defines ARS as requiring irradiation of all or most of the body by penetrating radiation delivered over a short time. Partial-body injuries frequently do **not** produce the classic syndrome because the bone marrow and other critical organs are spared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/hcp/clinical-guidance/ars.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdc.gov">[CDC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Acute Radiation Syndrome: Information for Clinicians &#124; Radiation Emergencies &#124; CDCApril 23, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 23, 2024</span></span></span>

Publicly available summaries of Cash's hospital records do not document the hallmark laboratory pattern expected after clinically significant whole-body exposure, such as:

* progressive depression of white blood cell counts due to bone marrow injury;
* severe thrombocytopenia causing bleeding tendencies;
* dose estimation based on [serial blood counts]({{ 'blood-counts-a88e65/' | relative_url }}) or chromosome analysis;
* diagnosis of acute radiation syndrome by treating physicians.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://remm.hhs.gov/cutaneoussyndrome.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: remm.hhs.gov">[remm.hhs.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">remm.hhs.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">REMMCutaneous Radiation Syndrome</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Radiation Emergency Medical Management...</span></span></span>

Instead, the contemporaneous dermatological assessment reportedly diagnosed [cellulitis]({{ 'cellulitis/' | relative_url }}) with secondary oedema rather than radiation injury. While that diagnosis could theoretically have been incorrect, it illustrates that clinicians treating Cash did not document unequivocal evidence of classical ARS in the surviving records.

This absence does not exclude every possible radiation scenario. It does, however, weaken the case for a high-dose uniform whole-body exposure of the type required to produce textbook [radiation sickness]({{ 'radiation-claim/' | relative_url }}).

## Why the dose distribution cannot be reconstructed

The greatest obstacle to evaluating the radiation hypothesis is the lack of dosimetric evidence.

Modern investigations of suspected accidental radiation exposure attempt to estimate absorbed dose using multiple independent methods, including:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* serial complete blood counts;
* chromosome aberration (cytogenetic) analysis;
* physical dosimetry from the scene;
* mapping of skin injuries;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/signs-symptoms/cutaneous-radiation-injury.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdc.gov">[cdc.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cutaneous radiation injury</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">cutaneous radiation injury</span></span></span>
* photographs documenting progression over time;
* measurements of contamination or radiation sources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/hcp/clinical-guidance/ars.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdc.gov">[CDC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Acute Radiation Syndrome: Information for Clinicians &#124; Radiation Emergencies &#124; CDCApril 23, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 23, 2024</span></span></span>

</div>

None of these allows retrospective reconstruction in the Cash–Landrum case because no verified radiation source was recovered, no exposure measurements were made at the scene, and no detailed dose map of Cash's injuries exists in the surviving public record.

Even if one assumes radiation was responsible, fundamental questions remain unanswered:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Was exposure confined to the head and upper body?
* Was one side of the body irradiated more heavily than the other?
* Was the radiation penetrating enough to reach internal organs?
* How long did exposure last?
* What absorbed dose, measured in gray (Gy), reached each tissue?

</div>

Without those parameters, no reliable medical dosimetry can be performed.

## What this means for interpreting Cash's illness

Viewed through modern radiation medicine, a **localised exposure hypothesis** is medically more compatible with Cash's documented skin changes than a straightforward diagnosis of classical whole-body acute radiation syndrome. Local radiation injury can produce swelling, scalp damage and delayed hair loss without necessarily causing the profound bone marrow failure expected after high-dose whole-body irradiation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/hcp/clinical-guidance/cri.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdc.gov">[cdc.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cutaneous Radiation Injury (CRI): Information for Clinicians &#124; Radiation Emergencies &#124; CDCApril 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 17, 2024</span></span></span>

That observation should not be confused with proof that radiation occurred. The surviving evidence establishes neither the presence of an ionising radiation source nor the pattern of any absorbed dose. Instead, it narrows the medical discussion: if radiation played any role in Betty Cash's illness, the available clinical record fits a **partial-body or local injury model** more readily than classical whole-body ARS, while leaving the underlying cause unresolved because the exposure itself cannot be reconstructed from the surviving documentation.

    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
