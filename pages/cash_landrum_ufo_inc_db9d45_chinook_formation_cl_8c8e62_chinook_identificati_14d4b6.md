## Introduction

One of the most persistent claims associated with the Cash–Landrum incident is that the unidentified object was accompanied by a formation of **CH-47 Chinook** helicopters. That description has become so common in later retellings that it is often presented as an established fact. The primary evidence, however, is more restrained. Contemporary witness testimony supports the idea that **some of the helicopters appeared to be tandem-rotor aircraft**, making a Chinook identification plausible, but it does not demonstrate that every aircraft in the formation was a CH-47. The distinction matters because it separates what the witnesses actually reported from the stronger conclusion that later entered UFO literature.

## What the witnesses actually described at the scene

The earliest detailed account comes from the August 1981 interview conducted at Bergstrom Air Force Base. During questioning, Betty Cash explained that the helicopters surrounding the object were unusual because they had "**two rotors**". She estimated that she counted **[23 helicopters]({{ '23-helicopters/' | relative_url }})**, while [Vickie Landrum]({{ 'vickie-landrum/' | relative_url }}) believed there were about 26, although both acknowledged that the circumstances made an exact count uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 1 of 2August 17, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 17, 1981</span></span></span>

Crucially, the interview does **not** begin with an assertion that all of the aircraft were CH-47 Chinooks. Instead, the description starts with an observable physical characteristic:

* helicopters with two widely separated rotor systems;
* a large number of aircraft around [the object]({{ 'the-object/' | relative_url }});
* observations made at night while the witnesses were frightened and coping with intense heat.

Those details represent the primary evidence. The identification of a specific helicopter model came later, after comparisons with military aircraft silhouettes and discussions with investigators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 1 of 2August 17, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 17, 1981</span></span></span>

This sequence is important because eyewitness testimony is generally considered more reliable when describing visible features than when assigning precise technical identities.

## How silhouette comparisons shaped the later identification

The strongest documentary support for the Chinook interpretation appears in the Army Inspector General memorandum prepared during the official inquiry. Rather than stating that all of the aircraft were known to be CH-47s, the memorandum says that **through the process of identifying silhouettes, some of the helicopters were determined to be twin-rotor, or CH-47 (Chinook), aircraft**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/cashlandrumsarran01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: the Cash-Landrum case, 1980, USAF memo 1/4, Sarran...</span></span></span>

That wording is noticeably more cautious than many later summaries.

Several factors likely encouraged investigators and later writers to favour the Chinook:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* The CH-47 has one of the most recognisable silhouettes in military aviation, with a long fuselage and large rotor at each end.
* It was widely operated by the US Army in 1980 and was familiar enough that silhouette comparison was practical.
* Few other American helicopters share the same tandem-rotor layout.

</div>

As the case became more widely discussed, the careful phrase "**some of the helicopters**" gradually evolved into claims that **all** of the helicopters were Chinooks. That shift reflects interpretation rather than new evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/cashlandrumsarran01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: the Cash-Landrum case, 1980, USAF memo 1/4, Sarran...</span></span></span>

## Why similar tandem-rotor aircraft remain possible

Recognising a tandem-rotor configuration is not identical to identifying a particular aircraft type.

During the period, another American tandem-rotor helicopter was also in military service: the **CH-46 Sea Knight**. Although smaller than the Chinook and used primarily by the US Marine Corps and Navy rather than the Army, it shared the same basic rotor arrangement. At night, especially against the glare of an intensely bright object, distinguishing between the two purely by outline would have been difficult unless additional identifying features were clearly visible.

Other factors also complicate precise identification:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* darkness reduced colour and detail;
* the witnesses' attention was repeatedly drawn to the luminous object rather than the aircraft;
* helicopters were reportedly moving around the object rather than remaining stationary;
* stress and physical discomfort can reduce accuracy when recalling fine technical details.

</div>

These considerations do not invalidate the observation of tandem-rotor helicopters. Rather, they limit confidence in identifying every aircraft to a specific model.

## The markings problem

The Chinook identification also encounters a separate difficulty arising from the reported markings.

[Betty Cash]({{ 'betty-cash/' | relative_url }}) told Air Force interviewers that she saw **"United States Air Force"** on the helicopters. Yet the CH-47 was an Army transport helicopter, not part of the US Air Force inventory in late 1980. During subsequent litigation, the government maintained that the Air Force did not operate CH-47s at the time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 1 of 2August 17, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 17, 1981</span></span></span>

This creates several possibilities, none of which can be confirmed:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the markings were misread under difficult viewing conditions;
* the aircraft type was correctly recognised but the service branch was mistaken;
* the aircraft were not actually CH-47s;

## Why the distinction still matters

The historical question is not whether Chinooks are a plausible interpretation—they are. Their distinctive tandem-rotor profile makes them one of the most reasonable candidates if the witnesses accurately observed large twin-rotor helicopters.

The issue is whether the evidence justifies the stronger claim that **the entire formation consisted of CH-47 Chinooks**.

The surviving documentary record does not reach that level of certainty. The witness testimony consistently supports the presence of helicopters with tandem rotors, while the Army memorandum limits the identification to **some** aircraft based on silhouette comparison rather than direct verification. No flight records, photographs, maintenance logs or crew testimony have emerged to confirm the precise composition of the reported formation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/cashlandrumsarran01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: the Cash-Landrum case, 1980, USAF memo 1/4, Sarran...</span></span></span>

Accordingly, the most evidence-based characterisation is that the witnesses described a large formation that appeared to include **tandem-rotor helicopters consistent with CH-47 Chinooks**, but the available record does not establish that every aircraft was positively identified as that specific model.
