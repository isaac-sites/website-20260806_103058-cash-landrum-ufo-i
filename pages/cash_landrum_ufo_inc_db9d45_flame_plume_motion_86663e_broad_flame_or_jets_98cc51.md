## Introduction

A persistent question in discussions of the Cash–Landrum incident is whether [the object]({{ 'the-object/' | relative_url }}) was reported to have **one broad flame** beneath it or **several separate exhaust jets**. The distinction matters because later illustrations often portray the object with a neat, symmetrical cluster of rocket-like nozzles, implying an observer could clearly identify multiple engines. The surviving witness testimony does not support that level of visual precision. Instead, [Vickie Landrum]({{ 'vickie-landrum/' | relative_url }}) repeatedly explained that the glare was so intense she could not distinguish individual flames. What she remembered was the appearance of a single broad column of fire, comparable in overall effect to the exhaust of a launching rocket, not a countable arrangement of separate jets.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 1 of 2August 17, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 17, 1981</span></span></span>

That difference is important because it limits what can legitimately be inferred from the witnesses' observations. The primary evidence describes an overwhelmingly bright, intermittent downward emission whose internal structure could not be resolved, making later technical-looking artwork more interpretative than documentary.

## What Landrum Could See Through the Glare

The clearest discussion of this issue comes from the recorded interview conducted at Bergstrom Air Force Base on 17 August 1981. During questioning, investigators tried to establish whether the object appeared to have one engine or several by asking how many flames could be seen.

Vickie Landrum's answer was notably cautious. She said there was **so much light** that she was effectively seeing only one flame-like mass. When pressed again about whether more than one flame was visible simultaneously, she replied that she had not been looking for separate flames because she was frightened. [Betty Cash]({{ 'betty-cash/' | relative_url }}) likewise said she was concentrating on escaping rather than studying the exhaust.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview...</span></span></span>

Several points emerge from this exchange:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Landrum did **not** testify that only one engine existed.
* She did **not** testify that multiple nozzles were visible either.
* Instead, she explained that the brightness prevented her from resolving individual sources within the luminous discharge.
* Cash similarly declined to identify any engine arrangement because her attention was focused elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview...</span></span></span>

</div>

This distinction is easy to overlook. The witnesses were describing the visual impression produced by an intensely bright object rather than making a technical observation about its propulsion system.

## Why the Rocket Comparison Should Not Be Taken Literally

Landrum frequently compared the downward emission to the flame beneath a rocket. Read in context, however, this was an analogy intended to communicate appearance rather than engineering.

Immediately before and after making the comparison, she emphasised that she saw the flame "come down" much like a rocket launch, but she did not describe discrete exhaust bells or identifiable nozzles. The comparison conveyed:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* a broad downward burst of flame,
* considerable brightness,
* repeated surges rather than a continuous burn,
* and an association between stronger bursts and upward movement of the object.

</div>

None of these statements establishes how many exhaust outlets existed, if any.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview...</span></span></span>

That nuance is significant because readers sometimes assume the rocket comparison implies a conventional clustered rocket engine. The original testimony does not make that claim.

## How Later Artwork Sharpened the Plume

Many later books, magazine illustrations and online recreations depict the Cash–Landrum object with a sharply defined conical exhaust emerging from a central point or from several evenly spaced jets.

These images have considerable visual appeal because they create a coherent mechanical picture. However, they also introduce a level of geometric certainty that is absent from the recorded witness interviews.

Contemporary retellings increasingly standardised the imagery into:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a symmetrical diamond-shaped craft,<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UJdhk3_DurE" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: youtube.com">[youtube.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">youtube.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Betty Cash, Vickie and Colby Landrum on witnessing a fiery diamond-shaped UFO,</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Cash-Landrum incident: traumatized &amp; physically ill after terrifying UFO encounter, Dec 29, 1980...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 1980</span></span></span>
* a clean conical exhaust,
* and a clearly organised propulsion arrangement.

</div>

The original testimony is much less exact. It repeatedly stresses overwhelming brilliance, intense heat and difficulty seeing details through the light. Later researchers examining the interview transcripts have pointed out that descriptions of the object's glow and its flames often became merged in popular retellings, producing artwork that appears more precise than the underlying observations justify.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.blueblurrylines.com/2013/12/flaming-ufo-examination-of-cash-landrum.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blueblurrylines.com">[Blue Blurry Lines]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blueblurrylines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blueblurrylines.com.</span></span></span>

The evolution illustrates a common process in eyewitness cases: repeated illustration can gradually replace uncertain visual impressions with crisp, internally consistent imagery that was never explicitly reported.

## Why Jet Count Matters to Propulsion Claims

Whether the witnesses observed one flame or several separate jets affects how confidently later commentators can discuss propulsion.

If multiple independent exhaust streams had been clearly observed, researchers could legitimately speculate about clustered engines, vectored thrust or other propulsion concepts. Instead, the testimony leaves the situation unresolved.

The evidence supports only a few limited observations:

* a downward luminous emission was reported;
* it appeared broad enough that Landrum perceived it as one flame-like column;
* the witnesses could not reliably distinguish separate exhaust sources because of glare;

## What the Testimony Actually Establishes

When the surviving interview is read carefully, the answer to the question "Was it one flame or several jets?" is more restrained than many later depictions suggest.

The witnesses consistently described a dramatic downward burst of fire-like light, but they did **not** provide a reliable count of individual flames. Vickie Landrum explicitly stated that the brightness prevented her from distinguishing separate jets, while Betty Cash acknowledged she was not analysing the exhaust at all. Their testimony therefore supports the existence of a reported broad luminous plume, but not a well-defined arrangement of multiple rocket-like exhausts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview...</span></span></span>
