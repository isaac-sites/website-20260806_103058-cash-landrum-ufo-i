## Introduction

One of the central medical disputes in the Cash–Landrum incident is whether [Betty Cash]({{ 'betty-cash/' | relative_url }})'s reported hair loss requires a radiation-based explanation or whether it could instead have resulted from a recognised medical disorder. Alopecia areata has been repeatedly proposed by sceptical investigators as a conventional alternative. The surviving public record, however, is incomplete. While later accounts state that Cash's [medical records]({{ 'medical-record/' | relative_url }}) identified alopecia areata, the original hospital documentation has not been widely published in full, making it difficult to evaluate precisely what her treating clinicians concluded or how confident they were.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

The question is therefore not whether Cash lost her hair—most accounts agree that she experienced substantial [hair loss]({{ 'hair-loss/' | relative_url }})—but whether the pattern and medical evidence point more convincingly to an autoimmune condition such as [alopecia]({{ 'alopecia/' | relative_url }}) areata or to radiation injury. On the publicly available evidence, alopecia areata remains a plausible alternative explanation, but it cannot be confirmed with certainty.

## What alopecia areata looks like

Alopecia areata is an autoimmune disease in which the immune system attacks actively growing hair follicles. It usually produces sudden, non-scarring patches of hair loss, most commonly on the scalp. The affected skin generally appears normal rather than burned or scarred, and characteristic "exclamation-point" hairs may be visible around the edge of bald patches. In some patients the condition progresses to extensive scalp or even body hair loss, while in others it resolves spontaneously.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/dermatologic-disorders/hair-disorders/alopecia-areata?ruleredirectid=747" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: merckmanuals.com">[Merck Manuals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">merckmanuals.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Merck Manuals Alopecia Areata</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Merck ManualsAlopecia Areata - Dermatology - Merck Manual Professional Edition...</span></span></span>

Several features distinguish alopecia areata from hair loss caused directly by radiation:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* It is an immune-mediated disorder rather than the result of tissue destruction.
* It commonly causes patchy rather than uniformly distributed hair loss.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.merckmanuals.com/en-ca/home/skin-disorders/hair-disorders/alopecia-hair-loss" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: merckmanuals.com">[merckmanuals.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">merckmanuals.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Alopecia (Hair Loss</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Alopecia (Hair Loss</span></span></span>
* It does not require a documented exposure to radiation or toxins.
* Diagnosis is usually clinical, although scalp biopsy may be used when the presentation is uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/dermatologic-disorders/hair-disorders/alopecia-areata?ruleredirectid=747" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: merckmanuals.com">[Merck Manuals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">merckmanuals.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Merck Manuals Alopecia Areata</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Merck ManualsAlopecia Areata - Dermatology - Merck Manual Professional Edition...</span></span></span>

</div>

Importantly, the word *alopecia* by itself does not identify the cause of hair loss. It simply describes the condition of hair loss. A diagnosis of *alopecia areata* is much more specific because it identifies a recognised autoimmune mechanism.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/dermatologic-disorders/hair-disorders/alopecia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: merckmanuals.com">[Merck Manuals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">merckmanuals.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Merck Manuals Alopecia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Merck ManualsAlopecia - Dermatology - Merck Manual Professional Edition...</span></span></span>

## Why clinicians may have considered it

The principal reason alopecia areata entered discussions of the Cash–Landrum case is the claim that Betty Cash's treating physician recorded this diagnosis in her medical notes. Later reviews by physician Gary Posner and other sceptical investigators argue that the available records attribute her hair loss to alopecia areata rather than to radiation injury. Those reviews also contend that several of Cash's other [reported symptoms]({{ 'reported-symptoms/' | relative_url }}) could have arisen from illness predating the alleged UFO encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

From a clinical perspective, alopecia areata would have been a reasonable diagnosis if physicians observed findings typical of autoimmune patchy hair loss and lacked objective evidence of radiation exposure. Dermatologists routinely distinguish among multiple forms of alopecia by examining:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the distribution of hair loss;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.merckmanuals.com/home/skin-disorders/hair-disorders/alopecia-hair-loss" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: merckmanuals.com">[merckmanuals.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">merckmanuals.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Merck Manuals Alopecia (Hair Loss</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Levinbook, MD, Hartford Dermatology Associates Last updated: Oct 2025 v792299 VIEW PROFESSIONAL VERSION GET THE...</span></span></span>
* whether the scalp shows inflammation, blistering or scarring;
* the presence of characteristic broken hairs;
* associated nail changes;
* and, when necessary, biopsy findings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/dermatologic-disorders/hair-disorders/alopecia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: merckmanuals.com">[Merck Manuals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">merckmanuals.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Merck Manuals Alopecia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Merck ManualsAlopecia - Dermatology - Merck Manual Professional Edition...</span></span></span>

</div>

Because alopecia areata is a recognised medical condition that occurs without unusual environmental exposures, a clinician would not need to invoke radiation unless the clinical evidence clearly supported it.

## What evidence would be needed to confirm it

The surviving public record is insufficient to determine definitively whether Betty Cash had alopecia areata.

A confident retrospective diagnosis would ideally require evidence such as:

* contemporaneous dermatology consultation notes;
