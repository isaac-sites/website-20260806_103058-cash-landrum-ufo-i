## Introduction

The medical records associated with [Betty Cash]({{ 'betty-cash/' | relative_url }}) are among the most frequently cited pieces of evidence in the Cash–Landrum incident because they document that she became seriously ill and later experienced substantial [hair loss]({{ 'hair-loss/' | relative_url }}). They do **not**, however, settle the central dispute over what caused that illness. The surviving documentation is incomplete in the public domain, much of what is known comes through later summaries rather than a fully published clinical file, and key specialist evidence that would be needed to identify the cause of the alopecia is either absent or unavailable for independent review. As a result, the records are valuable as evidence that a medical problem existed, but they have important limits when used to argue for radiation injury or any other specific diagnosis.

## What the surviving accounts document

The publicly described hospital records consistently indicate that Betty Cash was admitted to hospital in early January 1981 with significant medical complaints. Across both proponents and sceptics of the case, there is broad agreement that she underwent prolonged treatment and that hair loss eventually became a documented feature of her illness. The existence of [alopecia]({{ 'alopecia/' | relative_url }}) itself is therefore far less controversial than its interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

What makes the medical record noteworthy is that it goes beyond anecdotal recollection. According to later descriptions of the records published by investigator [John Schuessler]({{ 'john-schuessler/' | relative_url }}) and reviewed by subsequent commentators, clinicians documented scalp inflammation, facial swelling, blistering complaints and progressive hair loss during the weeks after the reported encounter. Photographs taken during the illness also appear to show marked scalp hair loss, reinforcing that this was not simply an oral claim made years later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufology-news.com/u/18672430/Ufology_News/The_Cash-Landrum_Incident_-_J.Schuessler.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufology-news.com">[Ufology News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufology-news.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufology News THE CASH-LANDRUM</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ufology News THE CASH-LANDRUM</span></span></span>

At the same time, later medical reviewers have emphasised an important chronological detail. Gary Posner's review of the available records notes that Cash's attending physician reportedly observed little or no significant hair loss on admission, with more substantial alopecia developing afterwards. If accurate, this indicates that the hair loss evolved during the illness rather than presenting fully formed at the first examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

That distinction matters because the records demonstrate **that hair loss occurred**, but the sequence alone does not establish **why** it occurred.

## Missing dermatology and pathology evidence

The principal limitation of the available medical evidence is not that no records exist, but that the publicly accessible record is incomplete.

The material discussed in books, articles and later reviews does not include a comprehensive dermatology file containing the sort of information that would normally allow retrospective assessment of alopecia, such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* detailed scalp examination describing the pattern of hair loss;
* specialist differential diagnoses;
* trichological testing of hair shafts;
* scalp biopsy or histopathology;
* serial dermatology follow-up documenting progression and regrowth;
* pathology findings excluding competing causes.

</div>

Schuessler himself noted difficulties obtaining complete hospital documentation, including missing laboratory material, and much of what later writers discuss comes from excerpts or summaries rather than publication of the entire clinical record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufology-news.com/u/18672430/Ufology_News/The_Cash-Landrum_Incident_-_J.Schuessler.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufology-news.com">[Ufology News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufology-news.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufology News THE CASH-LANDRUM</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ufology News THE CASH-LANDRUM</span></span></span>

Equally important is what dermatology consultants reportedly **did** record. Posner's review states that the dermatology consultation described [cellulitis]({{ 'cellulitis/' | relative_url }}) and swelling of the scalp and face, without documenting extensive skin loss or providing a specialist conclusion that radiation injury had caused the alopecia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

This absence should not be interpreted as evidence against radiation exposure by itself. Instead, it illustrates a more limited point: the surviving records do not contain the specialist diagnostic material that would allow later reviewers to determine the precise mechanism of hair loss with confidence.

## Why retrospective diagnosis remains uncertain

Retrospective diagnosis is inherently difficult when clinicians cannot re-examine the patient and must rely on incomplete historical documentation.

Several different processes can produce substantial hair loss after severe illness, including inflammatory disease, autoimmune alopecia, medication effects, physiological stress and radiation injury. Distinguishing among them usually depends on contemporaneous specialist observations rather than on photographs or general descriptions years later.

The Cash records therefore have two strengths and one major weakness.

Their strengths are that they document:

* a genuine period of illness requiring hospital treatment;
* significant hair loss developing after that illness.

Their weakness is that they do not contain enough published dermatological evidence to demonstrate which pathological process produced the alopecia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

This explains why different investigators have reached different conclusions while referring to essentially the same underlying documentation. Supporters emphasise the reality and severity of the illness, whereas sceptical medical reviewers focus on the absence of definitive diagnostic findings linking the hair loss specifically to ionising radiation.

## How strong is the medical record on hair loss?

As evidence that Betty Cash experienced substantial hair loss, the medical record is reasonably strong. Multiple later summaries, photographs and clinical descriptions point in the same direction, making it difficult to argue that the alopecia was entirely fabricated or invented after the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

As evidence for the **cause** of that hair loss, however, the record is considerably weaker.

The surviving documentation does not publicly provide:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* an independently reviewed dermatopathology report;
* definitive specialist attribution to radiation injury;
* laboratory evidence uniquely identifying radiation damage to the scalp;
* enough clinical detail to exclude recognised alternative explanations.

</div>

Consequently, the records support the limited conclusion that Cash developed medically documented alopecia after the reported incident. They do **not**, on their own, prove that radiation exposure caused it, nor do they establish that the reported aerial object was responsible. Those broader conclusions require additional evidence beyond what the surviving medical documentation can presently demonstrate.

      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });

      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {

    });

    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });

        });
      });

    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
