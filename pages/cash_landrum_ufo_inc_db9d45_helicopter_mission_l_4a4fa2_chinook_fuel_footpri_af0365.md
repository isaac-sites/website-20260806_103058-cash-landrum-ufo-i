## Introduction

If the Cash–Landrum witnesses had observed a formation of roughly 23 CH-47 Chinook helicopters operating together for even a relatively short period, the fuel demand alone would have represented a substantial military logistics effort. Heavy-lift helicopters consume fuel at rates far beyond those of light utility aircraft, and a formation of this size would require not only thousands of gallons of aviation fuel but also dedicated refuelling equipment, fuel handlers, parking space and documented stock movements. That does not prove such a mission was impossible, but it means investigators would reasonably expect evidence extending well beyond eyewitness reports. The fuel operation itself would become one of the largest physical footprints of the alleged flight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.army.mil/article/280363/aviation_refuel_planning_considerations_limiting_factors" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: army.mil">[army.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">army.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aviation Refuel Planning Considerations: Limiting Factors &#124; Article &#124; The United States ArmyOctober 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 17, 2024</span></span></span>

## Illustrative Hourly Fuel Consumption

Exact fuel burn depends on aircraft variant, payload, altitude, temperature and flight profile. A Chinook carrying heavy external loads burns significantly more fuel than one cruising lightly loaded. Even so, publicly available Army performance manuals and technical references consistently show that the aircraft operates on a scale measured in thousands of pounds of fuel per hour rather than hundreds. For illustration rather than precise reconstruction, consider a one-hour mission:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A single Chinook may consume roughly 1,300-1,600 US gallons during an intensive hour of operation, depending on conditions.

Applied across [23 helicopters]({{ '23-helicopters/' | relative_url }}), the formation would consume on the order of:

* approximately 30,000-37,000 gallons of fuel, or

These figures are illustrative rather than definitive, but they demonstrate the scale involved. A mission of this size is better understood as a mobile fuel distribution problem than simply a group of aircraft taking off together. ## Why Refuelling Becomes a Major Operation

The Chinook carries a large internal fuel load, allowing substantial endurance, but that capacity creates its own logistical demands. Technical manuals describe pressure-refuelling systems designed to fill multiple interconnected tanks rapidly, reflecting the expectation that heavy helicopters require specialised ground support rather than simple truck-and-hose refuelling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ch-47helicopters.tpub.com/TM-1-1520-240-10/css/TM-1-1520-240-10_57.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ch-47helicopters.tpub.com">[CH-47 Helicopters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ch-47helicopters.tpub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CH-47 Helicopters SECTION IV. FUEL SYSTEM</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CH-47 Helicopters SECTION IV. FUEL SYSTEM</span></span></span>

Modern Army doctrine places considerable emphasis on Forward Arming and Refuelling Points (FARPs)—temporary fuel sites established specifically to keep aviation formations moving. Planning guidance notes that heavy-lift helicopters impose particularly demanding fuel-flow requirements and that a refuelling point capable of servicing Chinooks requires fewer dispensing hoses per fuel source because each aircraft draws fuel so quickly. This increases both the equipment and personnel needed to sustain high sortie rates.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.army.mil/article/280363/aviation_refuel_planning_considerations_limiting_factors" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: army.mil">[Army]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">army.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aviation Refuel Planning Considerations: Limiting Factors &#124; Article &#124; The United States ArmyOctober 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 17, 2024</span></span></span>

In practice, refuelling a formation approaching two dozen heavy helicopters would involve:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* bulk fuel tankers or storage bladders,
* trained petroleum supply personnel,
* multiple pressure-refuelling stations,
* marshalling areas for aircraft waiting their turn,
* ground communications and safety supervision,
* fire protection and spill-control measures.

</div>

These are visible activities rather than administrative abstractions. They occupy space, require vehicles and involve numerous personnel working simultaneously.

## Parking, Ground Handling and Throughput

Fuel is only one constraint. Twenty-three tandem-rotor helicopters require extensive parking and manoeuvring space. Rotor clearance, taxi routes and refuelling safety distances prevent aircraft from simply being packed together.

Army refuelling planners specifically analyse the number of dispensing points required to prevent aircraft queuing. For Chinooks, fuel-flow limitations generally restrict each fuel vessel to servicing only a small number of heavy helicopters simultaneously, making the overall footprint larger than for formations composed of lighter aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.army.mil/article/280363/aviation_refuel_planning_considerations_limiting_factors" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: army.mil">[Army]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">army.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aviation Refuel Planning Considerations: Limiting Factors &#124; Article &#124; The United States ArmyOctober 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 17, 2024</span></span></span>

This means that even if all aircraft originated from a single installation, they would likely cycle through refuelling in serials rather than all at once. Ground crews would therefore remain engaged well before departure and after recovery, extending the operational window during which the mission might be noticed.

## Fuel Records and Stock Movements Investigators Would Expect

Military aviation fuel is not normally issued informally. Fuel deliveries, storage levels and aircraft servicing are tracked because aviation fuel is both expensive and operationally critical.

A mission consuming tens of thousands of gallons would likely influence several categories of documentation:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* fuel issue records from the supplying installation,
* inventory reductions in bulk storage,
* tanker vehicle dispatch logs,
* refuelling point activity records,
* aircraft servicing documentation,
* maintenance inspections associated with flight hours.

</div>

Not every document necessarily survives indefinitely, and archival completeness varies between units and eras. Nevertheless, the larger the fuel demand, the more independent administrative systems are likely to record some aspect of the activity.

Modern Army discussions of aviation logistics likewise treat fuel planning as one of the primary limiting factors governing helicopter operations. Rather than being an afterthought, fuel availability often determines how aviation missions are structured in the first place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.army.mil/article/280363/aviation_refuel_planning_considerations_limiting_factors" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: army.mil">[Army]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">army.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aviation Refuel Planning Considerations: Limiting Factors &#124; Article &#124; The United States ArmyOctober 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 17, 2024</span></span></span>

## What This Means for the Cash–Landrum Case

The reported [helicopter formation]({{ 'helicopters-977f69/' | relative_url }}) is often evaluated primarily through witness testimony and aircraft identification. The fuel requirement provides an independent way of assessing plausibility.

If approximately 23 Chinooks—or even a large proportion of them—had participated in a coordinated operation, investigators would expect not only flight crews but also a sizeable supporting logistics effort. The physical consequences would include substantial fuel consumption, organised refuelling activity and measurable movements of aviation fuel before or after the mission.

The Army's Inspector General investigation reportedly contacted relevant aviation commands yet did not identify records corresponding to such a large helicopter operation. That absence cannot conclusively establish that no helicopters flew, because historical records can be incomplete and eyewitness estimates can be inaccurate. However, the fuel footprint illustrates why a genuine 23-Chinook mission would be expected to leave traces extending well beyond the aircraft themselves. A formation of that scale would not simply appear in the sky; it would require an equally substantial logistical operation on the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
