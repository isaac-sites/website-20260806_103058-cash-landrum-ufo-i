## Introduction

The medical record surrounding the Cash–Landrum incident is considerably less complete than many later retellings suggest. Publicly available evidence consists of witness testimony, an August 1981 Air Force interview, references to hospital treatment, legal filings, photographs and later summaries rather than a complete set of contemporaneous medical records. As a result, some [reported symptoms]({{ 'reported-symptoms/' | relative_url }}) are well documented as having been described by the witnesses or observed by interviewers, while stronger claims—particularly that the witnesses were medically diagnosed with [radiation sickness]({{ 'radiation-claim/' | relative_url }})—are far less securely documented. The distinction between what was directly observed, what was recorded in medical settings, and what later authors inferred is essential to understanding the evidential strength of the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Transcript, Cash-Landrum InterviewJune 10, 2002...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 10, 2002</span></span></span>

The most consistently documented facts are that Betty Cash, [Vickie Landrum]({{ 'vickie-landrum/' | relative_url }}) and [Colby Landrum]({{ 'colby-landrum/' | relative_url }}) all reported becoming ill after the alleged encounter, and that Betty Cash sought extensive hospital treatment. ### Betty Cash's own description

Cash described becoming ill within a short time after the encounter and later developing severe swelling, blistering, weakness, dehydration and prolonged illness. She also stated that doctors performed extensive testing without identifying a condition that explained her symptoms. During questioning she specifically identified Parkway Hospital and her physician when discussing her treatment. Those statements document what she reported to investigators; they are not themselves independent medical conclusions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 1 of 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 1 of 2...</span></span></span>

One of the strongest pieces of documentary evidence is therefore not a diagnosis but Cash's own recorded statement that she underwent extensive medical evaluation and hospitalisation.

### Vickie Landrum's interview

Landrum's interview presents a noticeably milder clinical picture. She discussed becoming ill after the event, explained that she had not previously experienced significant health problems, and later described ongoing financial difficulty obtaining medical care. She also referred to treating Colby largely at home because doctors had not identified an effective explanation or treatment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 2 of 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 2 of 2...</span></span></span>

### Colby Landrum's interview

The interviewers questioned seven-year-old Colby separately. He acknowledged a facial blister, explained that it tended to reopen after sun exposure, and reported occasional continuing stomach problems. His answers are brief and often uncertain, but they do document that interviewers themselves asked about specific physical findings rather than relying solely on later recollections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 2 of 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 2 of 2...</span></span></span>

## Hospital treatment is documented, but the clinical record is incomplete

One of the clearest documented facts is that Betty Cash received hospital treatment. Multiple sources agree that she was admitted to Parkway Hospital after her condition deteriorated, although published accounts differ slightly regarding the exact duration and sequence of admissions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

The evidential limitation is equally important: the public record does not contain a complete hospital chart documenting every examination, laboratory result, consultation and discharge diagnosis. Instead, later writers frequently rely on:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* references made during the Air Force interview;
* descriptions contained in legal filings;
* quotations from selected medical documents;
* photographs taken during recovery;
* retrospective accounts published by UFO investigators.

</div>

Because the underlying clinical file has never been fully available in the public domain, many precise medical claims cannot be independently verified from original hospital documentation alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Transcript, Cash-Landrum InterviewJune 10, 2002...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 10, 2002</span></span></span>

## Which claims are supported only by later summaries?

The best-known version of the case often contains stronger medical conclusions than those directly supported by surviving documentation.

Frequently repeated claims include:

* that doctors formally diagnosed acute radiation sickness;
* that hospital physicians concluded radiation exposure caused the illness;
* that [medical records]({{ 'medical-record/' | relative_url }}) definitively proved exposure to ionising radiation.

Publicly available evidence does not clearly support these stronger formulations. Later books, documentaries and UFO literature sometimes present these conclusions as established facts, but the publicly accessible Air Force transcript does not contain physicians making such diagnoses, and complete hospital records supporting those conclusions have not been released.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Transcript, Cash-Landrum InterviewJune 10, 2002...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 10, 2002</span></span></span>

## Where observation ends and interpretation begins

The strongest evidence in the case concerns the existence of illness, not its cause.

Reasonably well documented are:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* witness reports of acute symptoms;
* Betty Cash's hospitalisation;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[cufon.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 1 of 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 1 of 2...</span></span></span>

* visible skin changes and later hair loss reported by the witnesses and shown in photographs discussed in later publications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Transcript, Cash-Landrum InterviewJune 10, 2002...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 10, 2002</span></span></span>

</div>

Much weaker are claims that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* radiation exposure was medically confirmed;
* the symptoms uniquely indicated ionising radiation;
* alternative medical explanations were conclusively excluded.

## Why evidence quality matters in this case

The Cash–Landrum incident illustrates how an unusual medical narrative can evolve over decades. The historical record supports the conclusion that the witnesses consistently reported genuine physical symptoms and that Betty Cash underwent significant medical treatment. It is much less complete when readers ask exactly what physicians observed, what laboratory findings were recorded or what formal diagnoses were entered into hospital records.

Consequently, the most defensible historical description is that the witnesses' illnesses are documented primarily through contemporaneous testimony, references to hospital care and partial documentary records, while the frequently repeated assertion that radiation sickness was medically established rests on later interpretations rather than a complete, publicly available clinical record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[cufon.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Transcript, Cash-Landrum InterviewJune 10, 2002...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 10, 2002</span></span></span>
