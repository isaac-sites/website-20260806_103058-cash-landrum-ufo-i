## Introduction

[Vickie Landrum]({{ 'vickie-landrum/' | relative_url }})'s reported injuries occupy a distinctive place within the Cash–Landrum incident because they combined physical signs that were observed by others with later claims that relied largely on her own recollection. Unlike Betty Cash, whose illness dominated later accounts, Vickie described a pattern of arm blisters, lingering scars, complete fingernail loss and delayed hair loss that she believed followed the December 1980 encounter. The available record indicates that some aspects of her condition—notably scars and blistering on her arms—were directly noted during an August 1981 interview with US Air Force personnel, while other reported effects, such as the loss of all her fingernails and later hair changes, were documented primarily through [her testimony]({{ 'her-testimony/' | relative_url }}) rather than independent medical publications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 1 of 2August 17, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 17, 1981</span></span></span>

## How severe were Vickie Landrum's injuries?

Vickie consistently portrayed her illness as serious but substantially less disabling than [Betty Cash]({{ 'betty-cash/' | relative_url }})'s. She told Air Force interviewers that she suffered diarrhoea, persistent eye irritation, blistering on her arms and hands, and recurring skin problems that flared after sun exposure. She explained that, during the first weeks after the incident, she delayed seeking treatment because she was caring for both Betty Cash and her young grandson Colby, whom she described as being more severely affected than herself at that stage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie &amp; Colby Landrum, Part 1 of 2August 17, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 17, 1981</span></span></span>

## Arm lesions noted during the interview

Among Vickie's [reported symptoms]({{ 'reported-symptoms/' | relative_url }}), the arm lesions have the strongest contemporary support because they were discussed while investigators were examining her in person.

During the August 1981 Bergstrom Air Force Base interview:

* Vickie pointed to visible scars on her arms and said that some had begun as large blisters.
* She stated that the blisters eventually healed but left scars and hard "knots".
* She recalled that physicians had initially asked whether poison ivy or poison oak might explain the lesions.
* One interviewer described seeing several scars and blisters on the lower portions of her arms and hands, along with a dark purple bruise-like mark on her left hand that Vickie said had recently blistered after prolonged sunlight exposure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview...</span></span></span>

These observations are significant because they distinguish between symptoms merely reported by the witness and physical findings that interviewers themselves recorded. However, the interview did not establish the medical cause of the lesions. The visible marks confirmed that skin abnormalities existed, not that they resulted from radiation or from the reported aerial encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview...</span></span></span>

## Claims of nail loss and delayed hair loss

Two of Vickie's most striking claims were that she lost all of her fingernails and later experienced substantial [hair loss]({{ 'hair-loss/' | relative_url }}).

According to her testimony, her fingernails "came completely off" before eventually growing back. She told the Air Force investigators that she had preserved every detached nail, individually labelled, because she hoped scientists would examine them. The interviewers asked whether she had already provided them to researchers, and she replied that she had not yet done so but intended to do so. The publicly available record does not document any published scientific analysis of those preserved nails.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview...</span></span></span>

Vickie also distinguished her hair loss from Betty Cash's by describing a delayed onset. She said her hair did not begin falling out until roughly one month to six weeks after the incident. She further stated that the regrown hair differed markedly in texture from before, describing it as no longer soft or easily manageable and expressing embarrassment about her altered appearance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview...</span></span></span>

## Why these symptoms remain difficult to interpret

Supporters of the Cash–Landrum case have often argued that Vickie's blistering, nail changes and delayed hair loss resemble injuries associated with significant environmental exposure. Skeptical investigators, however, note that resemblance alone does not establish a specific cause. They point out that the medical evidence publicly available for Vickie's condition is incomplete, that some claims rest almost entirely on witness testimony, and that proposed explanations—including ionising radiation—face substantial medical and evidential difficulties.
