## Introduction

The reported stopping point on FM 1485 is one of the most important yet least certain locations in the Cash–Landrum incident. Although the witnesses consistently placed the encounter on the rural road between New Caney and Huffman, investigators never established the exact point where [Betty Cash]({{ 'betty-cash/' | relative_url }}) stopped her vehicle beneath the reported object. As a result, every later attempt to identify scorch marks, damaged vegetation, road alterations or other physical traces has rested on an estimated rather than verified location. This uncertainty affects not only claims about environmental evidence but also later efforts to reconstruct [the object]({{ 'the-object/' | relative_url }})'s position, estimate its height, or compare witness testimony with modern maps. The surviving record shows that the problem was not simply a lack of physical evidence; it was that the scene itself could never be fixed with confidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.blueblurrylines.com/2013/11/the-cash-landrum-incident-suppressed.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blueblurrylines.com">[Blue Blurry Lines]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blueblurrylines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Blue Blurry LinesBlue Blurry Lines: The Cash-Landrum Incident: The Suppressed Case FilesNovember 12, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 2013</span></span></span>

## How witnesses described the roadside area

The witnesses consistently described a narrow, wooded stretch of FM 1485 at night, bordered by tall pine trees and with very little artificial lighting. Their descriptions focused on the extraordinary object overhead rather than on ordinary roadside landmarks. They recalled stopping because the object appeared to block the road and radiated intense heat, but they did not record mile markers, property numbers or other features that could later identify the precise location.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufocasebook.com/CashLandrum1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufocasebook.com">[UFO Casebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufocasebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash Landrum1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO CasebookCash/Landrum Interview, Part 1August 17, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 17, 1981</span></span></span>

The circumstances made precise orientation especially difficult:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the encounter reportedly occurred after dark on an unfamiliar rural road;
* the witnesses were frightened and concentrating on the object rather than navigation;
* they resumed driving afterwards while continuing to observe the object and accompanying helicopters from changing positions;
* the route included bends, wooded sections and repeated changes in viewing angle.

</div>

These factors make it difficult to separate the exact stopping point from later observation points along the drive. What survives in testimony is therefore a remembered stretch of road rather than a surveyed location.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/cashlandrumint01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: the Cash-Landrum case, 1980, USAF interview part I...</span></span></span>

## What investigators found near Inland Road

When civilian investigators, including [John Schuessler]({{ 'john-schuessler/' | relative_url }}), revisited the area with [Vickie Landrum]({{ 'vickie-landrum/' | relative_url }}) in February 1981, the search centred on the vicinity of Inland Road. Contemporary accounts describe the investigators reaching the place where Landrum believed the vehicle had stopped rather than independently confirming it through surviving physical markers or measured coordinates. The visit produced a candidate scene based on witness recollection instead of a securely identified site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.blueblurrylines.com/2013/11/the-cash-landrum-incident-suppressed.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blueblurrylines.com">[Blue Blurry Lines]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blueblurrylines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Blue Blurry LinesBlue Blurry Lines: The Cash-Landrum Incident: The Suppressed Case FilesNovember 12, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 2013</span></span></span>

The timing also mattered. Roughly two months had passed since the reported incident before the principal field investigation began. During that period:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* routine weather could have altered roadside conditions;
* traffic and normal maintenance could have affected the road surface;
* temporary visual clues remembered by the witnesses may have disappeared;
* memories naturally became less precise after repeated retellings.

</div>

None of these factors proves that physical evidence once existed and vanished, but together they reduced the chances of matching remembered details to one exact location.

## Why the location remained disputed

One of the most significant problems is that different records describe different levels of certainty about the site.

Material reviewed by the Texas Department of Health recorded Schuessler explaining that the witnesses could identify only a general area on FM 1485—described as a straight stretch between a beer establishment and a highway warning sign—because of the late hour and their emotional state. That account portrays the location as approximate rather than fixed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.blueblurrylines.com/2013/11/the-cash-landrum-incident-suppressed.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blueblurrylines.com">[Blue Blurry Lines]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blueblurrylines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Blue Blurry LinesBlue Blurry Lines: The Cash-Landrum Incident: The Suppressed Case FilesNovember 12, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 2013</span></span></span>

Later publications by Schuessler presented a stronger conclusion, stating that separate visits by the witnesses led investigators back to essentially the same location and verified the scene. He also later described apparent heating or burning of part of the roadway. These later claims are more confident than the earlier statements recorded during the investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.blueblurrylines.com/2013/11/the-cash-landrum-incident-suppressed.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blueblurrylines.com">[Blue Blurry Lines]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blueblurrylines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Blue Blurry LinesBlue Blurry Lines: The Cash-Landrum Incident: The Suppressed Case FilesNovember 12, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 2013</span></span></span>

This difference does not necessarily indicate bad faith, but it illustrates why [the stop]({{ 'the-stop/' | relative_url }}) site remains controversial. Historians and later analysts must weigh:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* early investigative records made close to the event;
* later recollections published years afterwards;
* television reconstructions;
* retrospective interpretations that may have incorporated additional assumptions.

</div>

Because these sources are not fully consistent, the exact stopping point cannot be treated as securely established.

## Why later maps cannot establish a precise point

Modern maps and satellite imagery often create an impression of precision that the original evidence does not support.

Although some later publications place the encounter just south of Inland Road or provide estimated coordinates, these locations are reconstructions derived from witness narratives rather than measurements taken on the night of the event. The original witnesses themselves struggled to identify the precise position on available maps during later interviews, demonstrating that even they could identify only the general area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/cashlandrumint01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: the Cash-Landrum case, 1980, USAF interview part I...</span></span></span>

This limitation affects several later analyses. Calculations of the reported object's:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* altitude,
* distance from the vehicle,
* apparent size,
* angle above the road, and
* relationship to surrounding trees

</div>

all depend upon knowing exactly where the vehicle stopped. A positional uncertainty of even a few hundred metres changes those geometric estimates substantially on a winding rural road.

For that reason, later map overlays should be viewed as illustrative reconstructions rather than established measurements.

## What the uncertainty means for the environmental evidence

The inability to identify the stop site with certainty also limits the value of reported physical traces.

Claims have circulated about burned pavement, distorted road markings and later resurfacing of the road. However, because the exact stopping point was never independently fixed, investigators cannot conclusively demonstrate that any observed road feature corresponded to the witnesses' stopping location rather than another section of FM 1485. Subsequent critical reviews have also noted that ordinary road maintenance and the generally poor condition of parts of FM 1485 complicate efforts to interpret later photographs or resurfacing as evidence connected with the incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.blueblurrylines.com/search/label/Road" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blueblurrylines.com">[Blue Blurry Lines]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blueblurrylines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blueblurrylines.com.</span></span></span>

The consequence is methodological rather than sensational: without a securely established scene, environmental observations cannot be confidently tied to the reported encounter.

## The lasting significance of the missing stop site

The uncertainty surrounding the FM 1485 stopping point is not a minor geographical detail. It forms one of the principal evidential limitations of the entire Cash–Landrum case. The witnesses consistently identified the same general roadway, but no contemporaneous survey, photograph, coordinate or enduring landmark fixed the exact place where the vehicle allegedly stopped.

As a result, the location remains a memory-based reconstruction rather than a demonstrably recovered scene. That distinction is crucial when evaluating later claims about physical traces, mapping the reported flight path, or assessing competing explanations for what occurred on FM 1485.
