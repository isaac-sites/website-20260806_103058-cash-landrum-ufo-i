## Introduction

The reported diamond shape of the object in the Cash–Landrum incident is one of the strongest arguments both for and against a [secret-aircraft]({{ 'secret-aircraft/' | relative_url }}) explanation. On one hand, unusual military prototypes have sometimes featured unconventional outlines. On the other, the specific silhouette eventually described by the witnesses—an upright, vertically deep diamond with truncated ends, a band of [blue lights]({{ 'blue-lights/' | relative_url }}) around its middle, and flames emerging from its lower point—fits poorly with the configuration of known aircraft from the late 1970s and early 1980s. The problem is compounded by the witnesses' own early admission that the intense glare made the object's true outline difficult to distinguish. That combination means the reported shape cannot be treated as a precise technical drawing, yet even allowing for visual distortion, it remains difficult to match convincingly with documented experimental airframes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

## What the witnesses actually said about the silhouette

The commonly reproduced image of the Cash–Landrum object did not emerge immediately. In interviews conducted only weeks after the incident, Betty Cash stated that the lights were too bright for her to determine the object's exact shape, while [Vickie Landrum]({{ 'vickie-landrum/' | relative_url }}) similarly said that she could not clearly make it out. At that stage, only [Colby Landrum]({{ 'colby-landrum/' | relative_url }}) insisted that it resembled a large diamond. Months later, during an interview at Bergstrom Air Force Base, Betty Cash sketched what she called "a diamond," and Vickie Landrum agreed with the drawing while adding that flames projected from its lower end.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because the identification problem depends heavily on how literally the geometry is interpreted. If the witnesses saw only a brilliant source surrounded by glare, the apparent outline could have differed substantially from the underlying object.

## How glare and viewing angle can alter aircraft shapes

[Bright light]({{ 'bright-light/' | relative_url }}) sources routinely distort perceived shape, especially at night. Human observers tend to perceive the luminous envelope surrounding an intensely bright object rather than its structural edges. Several effects are relevant to the Cash–Landrum descriptions:

* **Glare enlarges apparent size.** Intense light can obscure external features and make compact objects appear much larger.
* **Flames alter perceived geometry.** Repeated downward bursts of bright exhaust may visually merge with [the object]({{ 'the-object/' | relative_url }}) itself, changing the apparent outline from one moment to the next.
* **Limited visual references reduce accuracy.** Against a dark rural sky with few background cues, estimating shape, depth and orientation becomes significantly more difficult.
* **Viewing angle changes apparent form.** A body viewed from below or at an oblique angle can appear taller or narrower than it really is, particularly if only illuminated portions remain visible.

These well-understood perceptual effects mean investigators cannot assume that the reported diamond represents the exact external structure. Instead, it is better viewed as the witnesses' best reconstruction after observing an extremely bright object under difficult conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

Importantly, however, visual distortion has limits. Glare may soften edges, enlarge bright regions or conceal details, but it does not automatically transform ordinary aircraft into convincing upright diamonds. Any aircraft hypothesis must therefore explain both the possibility of perceptual distortion and why the witnesses consistently settled on an unusually vertical geometric form.

## Why known experimental airframes remain weak matches

The secret-aircraft hypothesis gains credibility from the fact that numerous classified American aerospace projects remained hidden during the period. Aircraft such as Have Blue, the F-117 demonstrators and later Tacit Blue all showed that radically unconventional designs could exist without public knowledge.

The reported Cash–Landrum silhouette nevertheless differs from these programmes in several important respects.

First, most experimental aircraft still followed conventional aerodynamic layouts. Even stealth designs with faceted surfaces retained a broadly horizontal fuselage, wings and stabilising surfaces. None resembled a tall, vertically oriented diamond apparently hovering over a road.

Second, lifting-body research vehicles and flying-wing concepts also remained horizontally oriented because atmospheric flight depends on generating lift across broad surfaces. A narrow upright diamond would be aerodynamically inefficient if interpreted literally as the vehicle's primary body.

Third, vertical-take-off concepts provide little help. Programmes such as the Avrocar demonstrated that unconventional hovering aircraft could be built, but their external geometry was circular rather than diamond-shaped, and their technical difficulties became apparent at only modest hover heights. They do not resemble the reported Cash–Landrum configuration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

Even allowing for classified projects whose details remain unknown, aerospace historians have identified no documented American prototype from the period combining:

* prolonged low-altitude hovering;

The issue is therefore cumulative. Individual features may resemble isolated experimental technologies, but their combination does not correspond closely to any known aircraft family.

## Could the reported diamond have been something else?

One possibility is that the witnesses accurately observed the behaviour but not the structure. If an aircraft were enveloped by exceptionally bright exhaust, plasma, combustion products or reflected light, observers might reconstruct the illuminated region as a geometric object rather than the airframe itself.

This idea explains why early testimony expressed uncertainty about the silhouette while later recollections converged on a more definite shape. It also fits the fact that the reported flames formed a major visual feature throughout the encounter.

The difficulty is that this remains speculative. No identified propulsion system from the period is known to have produced a stable, vertically elongated luminous envelope capable of consistently presenting itself as a large upright diamond while hovering at treetop height. Consequently, the perceptual explanation reduces confidence in the exact geometry but does not generate a convincing aircraft identification.

## Why the diamond remains an unresolved identification problem

The diamond shape neither proves nor disproves a secret military origin. Instead, it exposes the central weakness of the aircraft hypothesis.

The earliest testimony suggests the witnesses did not have an unobstructed view of the object's true outline because of overwhelming brightness. That caution prevents investigators from treating later illustrations as precise engineering evidence. Yet once reasonable allowances are made for glare, perspective and memory, the remaining description still fails to correspond well with documented American experimental aircraft of the era.

For that reason, the reported diamond should be regarded as evidence with two simultaneous implications: it is too visually uncertain to serve as a definitive blueprint, but it is also too unusual to provide a straightforward match with known aerospace designs. Within the broader debate over secret aircraft, this tension remains one of the strongest reasons why the Cash–Landrum object has resisted conventional identification.

  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
