## Introduction

One of the best-known features of the Cash–Landrum incident is the claim that [the object]({{ 'the-object/' | relative_url }}) was diamond-shaped. However, the documentary record shows that [Vickie Landrum]({{ 'vickie-landrum/' | relative_url }})'s confidence in that specific outline was not constant from the beginning. Early recorded statements indicate uncertainty about the object's exact shape because of its intense brightness. Months later, during a formal Air Force interview, Landrum endorsed and signed the now-famous diamond sketch. That evolution does not, by itself, demonstrate either fabrication or accuracy. Instead, it illustrates how a witness's visual description can become more definite as memories are revisited, discussed and translated into drawings. The distinction is important because Landrum's descriptions of the object's brilliant light, intense heat, flames and frightening presence remained comparatively stable, while her certainty about its outline became stronger over time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

## Her earliest uncertainty about the object's outline

The earliest documented accounts provide the strongest evidence that Landrum initially struggled to identify the object's shape.

In a recorded account transcribed roughly a month after the incident, Landrum reportedly explained that the light was too bright for her to distinguish the object's outline clearly. According to the same early material, she said, "Colby swore it looked like a big diamond. I couldn't tell." [Betty Cash]({{ 'betty-cash/' | relative_url }}) likewise stated that the brightness prevented her from determining the object's form with confidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gpposner.com/Cash-Landrum-chapter.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gpposner.com">[gpposner.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gpposner.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gpposner.com.</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because it separates two different observations:

* **Stable sensory impressions:** extreme brightness, [downward flames]({{ 'downward-flame/' | relative_url }}), intense heat and fear.
* **Uncertain visual geometry:** the precise outline of the craft.

The earliest evidence therefore suggests that Landrum's inability to define the silhouette was itself part of her original testimony, rather than an omission discovered later. The brightness that allegedly made the object remarkable also limited her ability to perceive its exact edges.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gpposner.com/Cash-Landrum-chapter.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gpposner.com">[gpposner.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gpposner.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gpposner.com.</span></span></span>

## Colby's role in the diamond comparison

The diamond comparison appears to have entered the record first through seven-year-old [Colby Landrum]({{ 'colby-landrum/' | relative_url }}) rather than through Vickie herself.

In the early transcript, Landrum specifically attributed the comparison to Colby, saying that he insisted it resembled a large diamond while she personally could not be certain because of the glare. That detail is significant because later retellings often compress the three witnesses into a single unified description, obscuring who first supplied the famous comparison.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gpposner.com/Cash-Landrum-chapter.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gpposner.com">[gpposner.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gpposner.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gpposner.com.</span></span></span>

This does not necessarily imply that Landrum merely adopted Colby's description. Witnesses who observe the same event commonly compare recollections afterwards, especially when one aspect of the scene was difficult to perceive directly. Modern research on eyewitness memory shows that discussion among witnesses can make later recollections more organised and internally consistent without requiring deliberate deception. In the Cash–Landrum case, however, the surviving documents allow readers to see that the attribution shifted from "Colby thinks it was diamond-shaped" towards "we saw a diamond-shaped object."<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gpposner.com/Cash-Landrum-chapter.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gpposner.com">[gpposner.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gpposner.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gpposner.com.</span></span></span>

## When the diamond outline became explicit

By August 1981, approximately eight months after the encounter, Landrum's description had become noticeably firmer.

During the recorded interview at Bergstrom Air Force Base, investigators asked Betty Cash to draw the object "if it had a discernible shape." Cash sketched a diamond, and Landrum immediately agreed with the drawing, adding that flames should be shown projecting downward from the lower point. Both women signed the sketch. The interview transcript records Landrum's agreement with the outline while refining the depiction by indicating where the flames emerged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Computer UFO Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Computer UFO Network Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Computer UFO NetworkTranscript, Cash-Landrum InterviewJune 10, 2002...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 10, 2002</span></span></span>

The progression can therefore be summarised chronologically:

Approximate stageLandrum's description of shapeEarly recorded accountBrightness prevented certainty; Colby believed it resembled a diamond.Bergstrom interview (August 1981)Agreed the object was diamond-shaped and signed the drawing, adding the descending flame.

## The signed drawing and its evidentiary limits

The Bergstrom drawing has become one of the defining images associated with the Cash–Landrum case, but its evidentiary value is narrower than it is sometimes portrayed.

The sketch demonstrates that, by August 1981, Landrum and Cash jointly endorsed a diamond-shaped representation. It is an authentic historical document showing their later recollection. It does not, however, establish that they described the object with the same certainty immediately after the event. The earlier recordings reveal that both witnesses initially expressed greater hesitation about its exact outline because of the glare.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Computer UFO Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Computer UFO Network Transcript, Cash-Landrum Interview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Computer UFO NetworkTranscript, Cash-Landrum InterviewJune 10, 2002...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 10, 2002</span></span></span>

## What the change does—and does not—show

The documentary record supports a careful conclusion.

Landrum's strongest and most consistent claims throughout the available evidence concern the encounter's brightness, descending flames, extraordinary heat and the fear experienced by those in the vehicle. Her confidence about the object's geometric outline developed later, moving from uncertainty caused by glare to explicit endorsement of the now-famous diamond shape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gpposner.com/Cash-Landrum-chapter.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gpposner.com">[gpposner.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gpposner.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gpposner.com.</span></span></span>

That progression is important for evaluating the testimony because it encourages treating different parts of the account separately rather than assuming every detail remained equally stable. The evolution of the diamond description neither proves the encounter occurred exactly as remembered nor proves it was invented.
