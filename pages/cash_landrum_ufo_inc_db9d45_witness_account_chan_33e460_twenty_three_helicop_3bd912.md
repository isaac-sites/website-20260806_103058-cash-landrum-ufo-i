## Introduction

One of the most persistent details in the Cash–Landrum incident is the claim that the witnesses saw **23 helicopters** accompanying or surrounding the unidentified object. Unlike other elements of the story that became more elaborate over time, the number itself appeared early and remained remarkably stable in later retellings. That consistency makes it historically significant, but it does not make the count automatically accurate.

The available record suggests that "23" endured because it entered the case very early, was repeated in interviews and publications, and became part of the narrative before memories had diverged substantially. At the same time, the circumstances under which the count was made—a frightening night-time event involving moving aircraft, intense heat, emotional stress and intermittent visibility—mean that the figure cannot be treated as independently verified.

## The Hospital Recording and the First Count

The earliest documented appearance of the number comes from the witnesses' recorded statement made at Parkway Hospital in early February 1981, only weeks after the alleged encounter. [John Schuessler]({{ 'john-schuessler/' | relative_url }}) later reproduced portions of that recording in his investigation, noting that [Betty Cash]({{ 'betty-cash/' | relative_url }}) said she counted **23 helicopters** after the witnesses had driven farther down the road and stopped again. Importantly, this places the figure among the earliest surviving details rather than among later embellishments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://electronicsandbooks.com/edt/manual/Magazine/M/MUFON%20UFO%20Journal/MUFON%20UFO%20Journal%20-%20September%201983.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: electronicsandbooks.com">[Electronics and Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">electronicsandbooks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MUFON UFO Journal September 1983</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Electronics and BooksMUFON UFO JOURNALDecember 21, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1983</span></span></span>

Early summaries of the testimony are also revealing because they contain fewer later interpretive additions. In Schuessler's initial account, Betty described counting 23 helicopters with ordinary main and tail rotors and believed they were military, but she did not initially describe a formation composed primarily of Chinook transport helicopters or report reading specific military markings on every aircraft. Later versions of the story became more specific in those respects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.blueblurrylines.com/search/label/Vickie%20Landrum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blueblurrylines.com">[Blue Blurry Lines]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blueblurrylines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Blue Blurry LinesBlue Blurry Lines: Vickie LandrumFebruary 8, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 8, 2018</span></span></span>

This distinction matters. The stability lies chiefly in the **number**, not necessarily in every descriptive detail attached to the helicopters.

## How Later Accounts Preserved the Number

By the time of the taped interview with Air Force representatives at Bergstrom Air Force Base in August 1981, the count of 23 had become a recognised part of the witnesses' narrative. When questioned, Betty Cash again stated that she had counted 23 helicopters after stopping farther along the road. However, the interview also contains an important qualification that is often overlooked.

When an Air Force officer confirmed the figure, Cash replied that **23 was her count**, while [Vickie Landrum]({{ 'vickie-landrum/' | relative_url }}) believed she had counted **26**, adding that everyone had been frightened, overheated and ill, making small differences understandable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/cashlandrumint01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: the Cash-Landrum case, 1980, USAF interview part IJune 4, 1996...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 4, 1996</span></span></span>

That exchange demonstrates two things simultaneously:

* Betty consistently associated herself with a count of 23.
* The witnesses did **not** all remember exactly the same number.

The later public literature generally preserved Betty's figure because it was the most frequently quoted, even though the interview itself shows that the witnesses' recollections were not perfectly identical. Subsequent books, magazine articles and documentaries therefore reinforced "23 helicopters" as the canonical version of the story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://electronicsandbooks.com/edt/manual/Magazine/M/MUFON%20UFO%20Journal/MUFON%20UFO%20Journal%20-%20September%201983.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: electronicsandbooks.com">[electronicsandbooks.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">electronicsandbooks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MUFON UFO Journal September 1983</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Electronics and BooksMUFON UFO JOURNALDecember 21, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1983</span></span></span>

## What Night-Time Counting Can and Cannot Prove

A stable number should not be confused with an objectively verified count.

Psychological research on eyewitness memory shows that highly emotional experiences often preserve the overall impression of an event while reducing precision in estimating quantities, distances and durations. The Cash–Landrum witnesses described:

* darkness interrupted by an intensely [bright light]({{ 'bright-light/' | relative_url }});

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* moving aircraft seen from changing positions;
* repeated stops beside the road;
* extreme heat and physical distress; and
* considerable fear.

</div>

Those conditions are poor for accurately counting numerous moving objects.

The Bergstrom interview itself reflects this limitation. Betty treated 23 as the number she personally counted while simultaneously acknowledging that another witness arrived at a different total and attributing the discrepancy to the stressful circumstances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/cashlandrumint01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: the Cash-Landrum case, 1980, USAF interview part IJune 4, 1996...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 4, 1996</span></span></span>

The figure therefore represents a **remembered observation**, not an independently established inventory of aircraft.

## Why Twenty-Three Became the Canonical Number

Several factors explain why "23" survived while other aspects of the narrative evolved.

First, it entered the documented record relatively early, before years of repetition and publicity had reshaped many other details. Early-recorded memories often become anchors for later recollection because witnesses repeatedly rehearse the same account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://electronicsandbooks.com/edt/manual/Magazine/M/MUFON%20UFO%20Journal/MUFON%20UFO%20Journal%20-%20September%201983.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: electronicsandbooks.com">[Electronics and Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">electronicsandbooks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MUFON UFO Journal September 1983</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Electronics and BooksMUFON UFO JOURNALDecember 21, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1983</span></span></span>

Second, precise numbers are memorable. A specific figure such as 23 is psychologically more likely to be repeated than an imprecise statement such as "about twenty" or "many helicopters." Once investigators, journalists and later authors adopted the number, it naturally became part of the standard retelling.

Third, the [helicopter count]({{ 'helicopter-count/' | relative_url }}) became central to the witnesses' interpretation of the event. During the Air Force interview, Betty explained that the helicopters were the reason they sought government involvement, making their presence a key element in the legal claims that followed. That importance encouraged repeated discussion of the count itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/cashlandrumint01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: the Cash-Landrum case, 1980, USAF interview part IJune 4, 1996...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 4, 1996</span></span></span>

## What the Count Can—and Cannot—Support

The persistence of the number provides useful historical information, but only within limits.

It supports the conclusion that reports of **many helicopters** were present from an early stage of the case rather than being a late invention. The repeated appearance of the same approximate number across early interviews indicates that this feature formed part of the witnesses' core narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://electronicsandbooks.com/edt/manual/Magazine/M/MUFON%20UFO%20Journal/MUFON%20UFO%20Journal%20-%20September%201983.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: electronicsandbooks.com">[Electronics and Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">electronicsandbooks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MUFON UFO Journal September 1983</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Electronics and BooksMUFON UFO JOURNALDecember 21, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1983</span></span></span>

However, the count does **not** independently establish that exactly 23 military helicopters were present. No contemporaneous military records, radar data or independent civilian observations have verified that precise number. Later investigators searched extensively for corroborating evidence but did not produce documentation confirming a formation of exactly 23 aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thegalacticmind.com/the-cash-landrum-encounter-1980-dayton-huffman-texas/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thegalacticmind.com">[The Galactic Mind]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thegalacticmind.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on thegalacticmind.com.</span></span></span>

The most balanced reading is therefore that **23** is one of the most durable components of the Cash–Landrum testimony, yet its durability reflects the consistency of repeated witness recollection rather than external confirmation. The number remained stable because it was recorded early, repeated often and became embedded in the witnesses' shared narrative, while the conditions under which it was originally counted leave unavoidable uncertainty about its literal accuracy.
